Simplifying Rowing into a Rigid-Body Dynamical System in Plane-based Geometric Algebra

1. Force as a line

One of the most fascinating aspects of Plane-based Geometric Algebra (PGA) is that geometric objects, such as points and lines, also represent transformations, such as rotations and reflections. Can we imagine the "force" of rowing as a geometric object?

For a rigid body, we describe a force using a direction and a point of application (a starting point). A direction and a point determine a line. When we slide the point of application along this line, the effect on the rigid body stays the same. So here, let's treat a force as a line.

\[ F = P \vee \mathbf{d} \qquad (P \to P + t\,\mathbf{d} \;\Rightarrow\; F \text{ unchanged}) \]

2. Types of strokes

The stroke line can change along with the boat or counter-change along with it. Try the three modes: forward keeps the line fixed in the world, draw carries it along with the boat, and sweep moves it against the boat's motion.

\[ F_{\text{stroke}} = p\,\widetilde{M}\,L\,M, \qquad L_{\text{forward}} = L, \quad L_{\text{draw}} = \Delta\,L\,\widetilde{\Delta}, \quad L_{\text{sweep}} = \widetilde{\Delta}\,L\,\Delta, \quad \Delta = M_t\,\widetilde{M}_{t-1} \]

Here \(\Delta\) is the motion between two discrete states of the boat: the current state \(M_t\) and an earlier state \(M_{t-1}\) (in the demo, the pose saved when the stroke is released).

3. Streamline as a drag scale offset

Water resists motion differently along each direction of the hull. Streamline, lateral and rotational drag are separate scales applied to the velocity components.

\[ F_{\text{drag}} = -\left( k_s\,v_{01}\,\mathbf{e}_{01} + k_l\,v_{02}\,\mathbf{e}_{02} + k_r\,v_{12}\,\mathbf{e}_{12} \right)^{\star} \]

Why the dual? Velocity \(V\) is a bivector (a point in 2D PGA: a center of rotation, or an ideal point for pure translation), while a force is a vector (a line). They are different grades, so the drag must map one into the other, and the dual does exactly that. The minus sign makes the drag oppose the motion.

The dual also gives us a freedom: the choice of dual is artificial but decides which center the drag is measured about. In Ganja.js, the dual depends on the base, whose center sits at the origin (zero) of the boat's frame. In the next section we will move this center.

4. Where is the hydrodynamic drag center?

Kayak Hull Dynamics: Drag Center vs. Momentum Center

The relative positions of the Drag Center (Center of Lateral Resistance, CLR) and the Momentum Center (Center of Mass, CoM) dictate how a kayak turns, tracks, and stops rotating:

Recall that we can shift the dual's center as we wish. Here we choose an offset \(\delta\) along the boat's long axis (usually boats are left-right-symmetric): the velocity is first shifted to \(V'\), and the drag is then computed from \(V'\) exactly as before. This moves the drag center relative to the momentum center.

\[ V' = T\,V\,\widetilde{T}, \quad T = 1 + \,\delta\,\mathbf{e}_{01}, \qquad F_{\text{drag}} = -\left( k_s\,v'_{01}\,\mathbf{e}_{01} + k_l\,v'_{02}\,\mathbf{e}_{02} + k_r\,v'_{12}\,\mathbf{e}_{12} \right)^{\star} \]

Feature Whitewater Kayak Sea Kayak
Geometry CLR in front of CoM (forward bow volume, high rocker) CLR behind CoM (long keel, skeg/rudder, low rocker)
Directional Stability Unstable (actively tries to spin out) Stable (naturally weathercocks/tracks straight)
Initiating a Turn Effortless — water pressure on the bow amplifies any small angle into a carve Resistant — hull fights against being pushed off its straight line
Canceling a Turn Hard — forward torque keeps feeding the spin; requires active stern strokes or edge shifts Easy — water pressure on the stern passively pulls the boat back in line
Primary Advantage Rapid micro-adjustments, tight pivots, and snapping across eddy lines Effortless straight-line tracking over long distances in wind and waves
